## 1. Peptide / GLP-1 medication travel cooler — PURSUE

| # | Filter | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Landed cost ≤ ¼ retail | **Pass** (passive) — lands ~$6–11, retails $35–55 |
| 2 | Retail $25–60 | **Pass** (passive) |
| 3 | Demonstrates in 3 seconds | **Partial** — it's a case, not a spectacle |
| 4 | Not in Target/Walmart | **Pass** — still specialty |
| 5 | Under 1 lb, not fragile | **Pass** (passive) — ~0.5–0.8 lb |
| 6 | Zero regulatory surface | **Conditional** — see naming rules |
| 7 | No brand marks or patents | **Conditional** — see naming rules |

**Passive only.** Insulated case with gel packs: lands ~$6–11, retails $35–55,
weighs under a pound, nothing to break. The battery-powered active-cooling
versions land $35–50 and retail $100–180 — that's 3x at best, plus lithium
battery shipping restrictions, plus electronics failure rates, plus a much
harder return. Same category, completely different business. Take the passive one.

**Filter 3 is the weak spot, and the audience makes up for it.** A case doesn't
have inherent visual spectacle the way a fidget toy does. But "traveling while on
a GLP-1" is already an entire content genre with a highly motivated audience and
a natural urgency trigger — an upcoming trip. You're not selling a novelty to a
scrolling stranger, you're solving a specific logistics problem for someone who
already has it. That converts better than novelty, and it retargets well.

### The two naming rules — follow these exactly

**TikTok explicitly permits this product.** The prohibited-products policy bans
products using "GLP," "GLP-1," or similar in the title or branding, *except for
products clearly marketed as supportive products for people using GLP-1
medications.* A cooler is precisely that exception. You are inside the carve-out
by design — but you have to stay inside it.

1. **Never make a drug, weight-loss, or efficacy claim.** Not "works like," not
   "supports your journey," not before/after imagery, no implied comparison to
   prescription medication. April 2026 guidance tightened this. Sell the *case*,
   never the outcome.
2. **Never use the drug trademarks.** No Ozempic, Wegovy, Zepbound, Mounjaro on
   the listing, packaging, or ad copy — those are Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly
   marks. Describe by fit instead: "fits 3mL vials," "pen-compatible,"
   "medication travel cooler."

**One more, and it's the serious one:** be careful about temperature claims. If
you advertise "keeps cold 36 hours" and it doesn't, the failure mode isn't a
disappointed customer — it's someone's spoiled medication. Test your samples with
a cheap thermocouple logger, state a conservative number you've personally
verified, and never repeat the supplier's marketing figure. This is the one place
in this whole business where the downside is worse than losing money.

**Competition is real.** Etsy and Amazon already have dozens of sellers from
$8.99 basic sleeves up to $179 digital-display units. That's a sign of demand,
not a closed door — but differentiate on capacity, organization, and
travel-specific framing (TSA, cruise, road trip), not on cooling technology,
where the incumbents already compete.

## 2. Metal fidget toys — NON-MAGNETIC ONLY, AND THE MARGINS ARE THIN

| # | Filter | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Landed cost ≤ ¼ retail | **Pass** — lands $2–5, retails $20–30 |
| 2 | Retail $25–60 | **Marginal** — anchored at $15–25, hard to hold above $30 |
| 3 | Demonstrates in 3 seconds | **Pass, best of the three** — ASMR, tactile, made for the format |
| 4 | Not in Target/Walmart | **Fail** — Five Below, checkout lanes, everywhere |
| 5 | Under 1 lb, not fragile | **Pass** |
| 6 | Zero regulatory surface | **Fail if magnetic** — see below |
| 7 | No brand marks or patents | **Caution** — popular designs get enforced |

**The magnet problem is not hypothetical.** CPSC issued multiple 2026 recalls of
magnetic fidget products, including magnetic fidget sliders specifically. Swallowed
high-powered magnets attract each other through intestinal walls and cause
perforation, blood poisoning, and death. Recall remedies require full refunds and
customers physically destroying the product. If you are marketing to anyone under
12, CPSIA children's-product testing applies on top of that.

The federal Magnet Sets Final Rule was vacated in court and pulled from the CFR,
which sounds like relief and isn't — the mandatory toy standard is what these 2026
recalls actually cite, and it is still in force. Do not read the vacated rule as
permission.

**If you want this category:** non-magnetic mechanisms only — detent sliders,
clickers, spinners, ratchets — marketed explicitly to adults, listed as an adult
desk item, never in a kids' toy category. Even then you're fighting commodity
pricing against a hundred identical sellers.

**Honest read:** this is the product you'd have the most fun filming and the least
fun banking. Keep it as a content-skills training ground if you like, but it's not
the one to build on.

## 3. Mini fridges — DROP

| # | Filter | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Landed cost ≤ ¼ retail | **Fail** — freight destroys the ratio |
| 2 | Retail $25–60 | **Fail** — typically $60–150 |
| 3 | Demonstrates in 3 seconds | **Pass** — genuinely great on camera |
| 4 | Not in Target/Walmart | **Fail badly** — everywhere, and cheaper |
| 5 | Under 1 lb, not fragile | **Fail badly** — 8–20 lb, dimensional weight, freight |
| 6 | Zero regulatory surface | **Fail** — mains power, UL/ETL expectations, refrigerant |
| 7 | No brand marks or patents | **Caution** — the cute ones are often unlicensed character knockoffs |

Five of seven, and the failures are structural rather than fixable.

A 12-pound item from China frequently costs more to ship than the unit is worth,
and dimensional weight means you pay for the box's air. Then the return math
compounds it: a dead compressor means you refund the customer *and* eat return
freight you never priced in, so one failure erases the profit from several sales.
Small refrigeration units have meaningful DOA and early-failure rates.

This is a classic beginner trap precisely *because* filter 3 passes so well. It
films beautifully. That's what draws people in, and the freight math is what
takes them out.

## The pattern worth fixing

All three of these arrived the same way: you saw them because they were already
peaking on your feed. The instinct behind that is genuinely valuable — you have a
working sense for what stops a scroll, which is the skill most people starting
this business completely lack, and it cannot be taught by a checklist.

The problem is purely timing. By the time a product saturates your For You page,
the sourcing is picked over, ten sellers are running the same creative, and the
margin has been competed out. You're reading a *lagging* indicator with a *leading*
skill.

### How to catch the same kind of product earlier

- **Sort by recent, not by top.** Search the category on TikTok and filter to
  recently posted. Top-sorted results show you what already won, which is the
  same as showing you what's too late.
- **Track creator count, not view count.** A product going from 3 creators to 30
  posting about it is early. One with 300 creators and a dominant branded seller
  owning the search term is over. The window is roughly that middle band.
- **Check supplier velocity as confirmation.** On AliExpress, compare reviews in
  the last 30 days against lifetime orders. A high recent share means demand is
  accelerating *now*. High lifetime orders with stale reviews means you found the
  peak six months late.
- **Watch adjacencies, not the product itself.** The peptide cooler is a good
  example of the right shape: it doesn't ride the GLP-1 trend directly, it serves
  people the trend created. Accessories to a boom age better than the boom, face
  less competition, and dodge the regulatory surface of the thing they orbit. When
  a category takes off, ask what its users now need to carry, store, clean,
  organize, or protect.

### Filling out the shortlist

You need five candidates and you have one worth pursuing. Rather than name
products here — they turn over weekly and anything I list would be stale by the
time you order samples — run the four checks above across two or three
adjacencies you're already close to, and take the first three that clear all seven
hard filters. The discipline that matters is ordering samples for all of them
before you fall in love with any one.

Order samples of **three passive medication-cooler variants** from different
suppliers, ~$60 total, and log their real temperature-hold curves when they
